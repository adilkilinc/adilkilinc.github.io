About

I’m a PhD candidate in solid mechanics at LaMcube (Centrale Lille Institut and Université de Lille), specialising in finite-element simulation and the mechanical behaviour of metallic alloys. My thesis identifies local material parameters in polycrystalline 316L austenitic stainless steel by coupling digital image correlation (DIC) with large-scale Abaqus simulations on HPC.

I hold an Erasmus Mundus master’s in Advanced Solid Mechanics, studied in France, Belgium and Poland, and a bachelor’s in mechanical engineering from Istanbul. Alongside the simulation work I teach experimental mechanics labs at Centrale Lille, so I’m as comfortable with a tensile machine as with a solver.

Outside work: chess, ice and roller skating, and trekking.

Research

Differentiating DIC displacements gives a strain estimate that is noisy and satisfies neither equilibrium nor a material law. My thesis takes a three-level route instead, working below the grain scale rather than grain by grain.

  1. Macroscopic calibration

    Fit the tensile response of the whole specimen to set the reference elastic–plastic behaviour.

  2. Pixel-wise identification

    Identify a Ludwik–Hollomon hardening law at every DIC pixel, producing maps of initial yield stress and hardening coefficient.

  3. Finite-element reconstruction

    Impose the measured displacements on the boundary of a pixel-per-element Abaqus model and solve for interior strain and stress that satisfy equilibrium and the constitutive law together.

Software

DIC-based in-house FEM

Author

Finite-element reconstruction of stress and strain fields from DIC measurements. Each pixel becomes one plane-stress CPS4 element carrying its own measured yield stress and hardening.

The same boundary-value problem is solved two ways: an Abaqus pipeline that writes the input file and extracts ODB results onto a grid, and a standalone Python solver with a closed-form von Mises return mapping, an analytical consistent tangent and Newton–Raphson iterations with automatic cutback, validated against Abaqus.

Python, NumPy, SciPy, Abaqus/Standard. LGPL-2.1.

Finite element simulator

Author

A plane-stress finite element solver that runs in the browser: classic test specimens, loading with the mouse and a force–displacement curve, verified against reference solutions.

JavaScript, Canvas.

Kinematics-driven 316L strain reconstruction

Co-author, original solver

A research platform that grew out of the in-house solver, developed with Jean-François Witz: 3D constitutive laws under plane stress through MFront/MGIS, Forest–Rubin crystal plasticity, spectral solvers, DIC/EBSD registration and identifiability analysis.

Python, MFront/MGIS, NumPy, Numba.

inpEditor

Author

Assigns per-grain elastic and plastic properties in Abaqus input files, computes grain size from triangular meshes and applies a Hall–Petch law.

Python, Abaqus input files.
